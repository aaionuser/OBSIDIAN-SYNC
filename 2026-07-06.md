This experiment studies the concept of electrochemistry, which is the relationship between chemical reactions and electricity. Theoretically, a species—which can be an ion, atom, or molecule—that loses an electron(s) and increases in oxidation number undergoes the oxidation process. The species that is oxidized is called a reducing agent, or reductant, as it causes the other species to undergo reduction. Conversely, a species that gains electrons and results in a decrease in oxidation number undergoes the reduction process. The species that is reduced is called an oxidizing agent, or oxidant, as it causes the other species to undergo oxidation.

​Redox reactions can be spontaneous or non-spontaneous. A spontaneous redox reaction releases energy which can be used to perform electrical work. This can be accomplished through a galvanic cell, built by immersing two electrodes into two different electrolytes. The electrode at which oxidation occurs is called the anode, meanwhile the electrode at which reduction occurs is called the cathode. When the anode and cathode are connected, electrons flow through an external circuit, producing electricity. Apart from that, electrolytic cells are used to convert electrical into chemical energy. An electrolytic cell is made up of two electrodes immersed in one electrolyte, which can consist of molten salts, pure water, or an aqueous salt solution. During electrolysis, the cation is attracted to the cathode and is reduced, whereas the anion is attracted to the anode and is oxidized. Hence, it is important to predict the products of electrolysis using the E^\circ_{\text{red}} value.

​This specific experiment is separated into two parts. In the first part, a galvanic cell will be built using active electrodes immersed in zinc sulfate and copper sulfate solutions, and the cell potential will be measured. In the second part, two electrolytic cells will be built using inert electrodes immersed in two different aqueous solutions, and the products produced at the anode and cathode will be determined. Apart from that, the amount of substance formed at each electrode can be predicted based on Faraday’s first law, where the mass of a substance deposited at an electrode is directly proportional to the charge.

Colligative properties are defined as the properties of a solution that depend solely on the number of dissolved solute particles and not on their chemical identity. Vapor pressure lowering, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, and osmotic pressure are all classified as colligative properties.

In this experiment, the effects of adding a solute to a solvent on the freezing point and boiling point of the resulting solution will be explored to observe changes in phase transition behavior. At a molecular level, the added solute particles physically interfere with the organized arrangement of solvent molecules during the freezing process. As a result, a lower temperature is required to solidify the mixture, meaning the freezing point of the solution is lower than that of the pure solvent; this phenomenon is known as freezing point depression. Conversely, the presence of the solute lowers the vapor pressure, meaning a higher temperature is required for the solution to boil compared to the pure solvent, a phenomenon known as boiling point elevation.
